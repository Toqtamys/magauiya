What it does

A learning sandbox for structural pharmacology. Pick an organ on the 3D model, then a target with a real PDB structure. Next: an approved drug, your own molecule (SMILES / SDF / PDB) or analogues by substituent swaps. RDKit in WebAssembly computes MW, logP, TPSA, donors/acceptors, Lipinski and Veber rules, ESOL solubility and Tanimoto similarity to the native ligand. Real docking runs in MolModa or PocketDock — the constructor prepares the files.

  • ▸Interactive 3D anatomy with organ hubs
  • ▸25+ targets with PDB entries and native ligands
  • ▸Properties and drug-likeness rules (RDKit.js)
  • ▸Analogue generation and ranking
  • ▸Export for MolModa / PocketDock / Vina

Input requirements

  • •SMILES, SDF/MOL or a molecule PDB file
  • •Own target — a 4-character PDB code or a protein PDB file

How to use

  1. 1Open the constructor and pick an organ on the 3D model.
  2. 2Pick a target — the RCSB PDB structure loads with the pocket highlighted.
  3. 3Pick a drug, paste your molecule or generate analogues.
  4. 4Compare properties and similarity to the native ligand.
  5. 5Press “Dock” — files download and MolModa opens.

Limitations and common mistakes

  • !No physics docking in the browser: the preview shows the pocket and native ligand.
  • !Lipinski/Veber and ESOL are heuristics, not experiment.
  • !Educational and research purposes only.

What you get

  • →Property table, rule flags, similarity, ranked analogues
  • →Receptor and ligand files for docking
